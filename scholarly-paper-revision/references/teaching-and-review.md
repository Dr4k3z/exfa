- **Motivation before formalism:** the introduction connects approximation to the central limit theorem and explains its use before developing machinery (printed pp. 145–146; PDF pp. 1–2).

- **Limits explained:** discussion identifies regimes where an approximation degenerates and motivates alternatives (pp. 167–168; PDF pp. 23–24).

## Reviewer handoff

The review is an independent assessment of the **paper**, not a review of these skill instructions. When independent review is appropriate, available, and authorized, give a reviewer the relevant draft and sources. Provide:

- the user's task, intended audience, and any explicit structure or length requirements;
- the relevant draft source and rendered PDF, notation conventions, and bibliography;
- the actual source papers or accessible copies, including declared exclusions and access gaps;
- any experiment code and outputs supporting newly claimed numerical results.

Do not provide your intended verdict, assurances of correctness, suspected errors, or proposed fixes before its first assessment. Ask it to act as an instructor and scholar in the sources' field and to return location-specific findings with severity, supporting source evidence where relevant, and actionable revisions.

The review should address:

1. Are important terms defined before use, with a clear separation between assumed graduate prerequisites and material being taught?
2. Can the reader follow central arguments, including assumptions, intermediate steps, limiting regimes, and translations between papers' notation?
3. Does the synthesis preserve what each paper actually proves or observes, including incompatible assumptions and unresolved disagreements?
4. Is the sequence teachable, and is the depth proportionate to the topic's complexity and the stated prerequisites? Identify both missing essential explanations and expendable material. Can unnecessary abstraction, routine background, or repetition be replaced by a simpler explanation or worked example without losing accuracy? Judge sufficiency by the arguments and teaching needs, not by an invented word count or preferred length.
5. Are simulation and empirical claims traceable to actual evidence, with appropriate distinctions between illustration, reproduction, and new experiments?
6. Are the scope, attribution, and organization appropriate to the manuscript and its intended audience?

Ask for a readiness judgment: ready for its intended purpose, ready after minor corrections, or substantial revision required. A positive verdict does not waive any identified correction.

## Revision and completion

Resolve material errors, unsupported statements, missing definitions, logical leaps, and unnecessary complexity. Record the disposition of substantive findings in working notes. For a disputed finding, check the source and give a reasoned resolution rather than dismissing it. Have the reviewer recheck materially changed arguments when needed; do not repeatedly request a fresh full review of unchanged text.

Compile and inspect the revised final paper, not the pre-review version. If a serious issue cannot be resolved within the authorized scope or resources, identify it and the needed next step; do not describe the paper as complete or ready for teaching. The final user response should briefly describe the review and resulting revisions without exposing internal deliberation.
